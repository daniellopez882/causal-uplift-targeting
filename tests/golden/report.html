Uplift decision report

3,000 customers, outcome purchase. Mean outcome by arm: control 0.3095, arm1 0.3410, arm2 0.3244.

The question

With a contact budget, which customers should receive which message so that the number of purchases caused by the campaign is as large as possible?

Recommendation

Budget 10%: target with forest-ed (sending arm1 to 10% of customers). Estimated 151.7 incremental purchases per 1,000 contacts (95% CI 67.2 to 227.0); 12.7 more per 1,000 customers than random targeting (95% CI 3.3 to 21.0).
Budget 30%: target with t (sending arm1 to 29%, arm2 to 1% of customers). Estimated 119.2 incremental purchases per 1,000 contacts (95% CI 54.1 to 191.7); 28.3 more per 1,000 customers than random targeting (95% CI 13.2 to 47.7).

Appendix: how these numbers were produced

  • Rows were split into 2 folds stratified by arm and outcome. Every learner was fitted on 1 folds and scored the remaining one, so each customer's score comes from a model that never saw them.
  • Budgets are applied inside each held-out fold: the top share of that fold by best predicted uplift is contacted, each with the arm of highest predicted uplift, skipping customers whose best predicted uplift is not positive.
  • Policy value uses inverse propensity weighting (IPW) and augmented IPW (AIPW). Propensities are known by design (the arm shares of a randomised test); the AIPW outcome model is a cross-fitted per-arm lightgbm model. Random targeting is scored in expectation over its randomisation.
  • Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over customers (50 replicates). Policy comparisons resample all policies jointly, so the gain against random targeting is a paired estimate. Fitted models are held fixed across replicates, so the intervals do not include refitting variance.
  • Assumptions: the arm was randomised (or its propensities are known), one customer's e-mail does not change another's outcome, and the future population resembles this one. The recommended policy is the best of several on the same data, so its estimate is slightly optimistic.
