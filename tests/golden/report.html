Uplift decision report

3,000 customers, outcome purchase. Mean outcome by arm: control 0.1948, arm1 0.2207, arm2 0.2502.

The question

With a contact budget, which customers should receive which message so that the number of purchases caused by the campaign is as large as possible?

Recommendation

Budget 10%: the best point estimate is response, but it does not separate from random targeting once the choice among the 3 learners is allowed for, so model-based targeting is not justified at this budget on this evidence. Gain against random targeting 5.89 per 1,000 customers (95% CI -6.34 to 17.89; simultaneous over the 3 learners -11.27 to 23.04); against sending arm2 to a random 10% 4.40 (95% CI -6.66 to 16.05; simultaneous -11.65 to 20.45).
Budget 30%: the best point estimate is t, but it does not separate from random targeting once the choice among the 3 learners is allowed for, so model-based targeting is not justified at this budget on this evidence. Gain against random targeting 15.48 per 1,000 customers (95% CI 0.74 to 34.47; simultaneous over the 3 learners -4.22 to 35.19); against sending arm2 to a random 30% 11.02 (95% CI -4.44 to 28.89; simultaneous -7.56 to 29.60).

Appendix: how these numbers were produced

  • Rows were split into 2 folds stratified by arm and outcome. Every learner was fitted on 1 folds and scored the remaining one, so each customer's score comes from a model that never saw them.
  • Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over customers (50 replicates). Fitted models are held fixed across replicates, so the intervals do not include refitting variance.
  • Budgets are applied inside each held-out fold: the top share of that fold by best predicted uplift is contacted, each with the arm of highest predicted uplift, skipping customers whose best predicted uplift is not positive. With unequal arm costs the budget optimiser makes that choice.
  • Policy value uses inverse propensity weighting (IPW) and augmented IPW (AIPW). Assignment probabilities: recorded with the data (known assignment probabilities). The AIPW outcome model is a cross-fitted per-arm lightgbm model. Random targeting is scored in expectation over its randomisation. Policy comparisons resample all policies jointly, so the gains are paired estimates.
  • Two policies use no model: random targeting (random customers, random arm) and, with more than one treatment, the best arm at random (random customers, all sent the arm with the highest average effect on these rows; choosing it with hindsight favours this reference).
  • The recommendation at each budget is the best of 3 learner policies on the same customers, so its gains over both no-model policies are judged on simultaneous 95% intervals (max-t bootstrap) that cover the gains of all 3 at once. Targeting is recommended only when both lower bounds are above zero. The point estimate of the chosen policy is still optimistic.
  • Assumptions: given the features, the arm received does not depend on anything that also drives the outcome (true by design in a randomised test), every customer could have received every arm, one customer's e-mail does not change another's outcome, and the future population resembles this one.
